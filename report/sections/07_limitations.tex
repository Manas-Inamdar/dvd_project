\section{Limitations and improvements}\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{What limits the accuracy}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{OFF channel-current models at hot corners.} The remaining gate-level error is concentrated at high temperature, where sub-threshold
        current becomes a large share of the leakage and the OFF models (median error 1--3\,\%) dominate (\cref{sec:errors}). The error of the ON
        gate-current models (0.1--0.6\,\%) is no longer the limit.
  \item \textbf{Corner-systematic error.} All gates of a circuit share one corner and hence the same systematic model error, which does not
        average out over a circuit. For larger circuits the error will therefore approach the per-corner error of the transistor models rather than
        shrink with the number of gates.
  \item \textbf{Node voltages from SPICE.} As the assignment prescribes, the internal node and output voltages are taken from the SPICE simulation of
        the gate; in the circuit, those of the isolated gate at the same corner are used. The method is therefore not yet a stand-alone replacement for SPICE.
  \item \textbf{Ignored loading and ideal logic levels.} The circuit prediction assumes inputs exactly at the rails and no loading between gates. For the
        parity checker this costs less than $10^{-4}$ (\cref{tab:cdecomp}); it may matter more for high fan-out or degraded logic levels.
  \item \textbf{Numerical floor of the reference.} The BSIM4 evaluation in ngspice is noisy below about $10^{-15}$\,A, so smaller currents (e.g.\ the
        OFF-state gate current) can neither be learned nor scored, and the mandated tolerances produced invalid operating points in 3.5\,\% of the gate runs.
  \item \textbf{Scope of the models.} The models are valid inside the sampled ranges only: $\Vdd\pm10\,\%$, $-40$ to $125\,^\circ$C, $W=1$--$8\Lmin$,
        $L=\Lmin$, process $\pm10\,\%$, and gate and body voltages at the rails as defined by the four cases.
  \item \textbf{Model selection informed by the composition.} Which currents receive rail-corner data and an ensemble was decided from the structure of
        the gate composition and from transistor-level hold-out errors. No gate or circuit SPICE value was used for training or selection, but the
        diagnosis that motivated the changes looked at gate-level errors, so the gate-level accuracy is not a fully blind estimate (\cref{app:assumptions}).
  \item \textbf{Assumptions not confirmed by the course staff} (\cref{app:assumptions}): leakage definition, gate widths, Kirchhoff-consistent
        composition, additional training data and the parity-checker architecture.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Possible improvements}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Targeted data for the OFF models}: more rail-corner rows at high temperature and low threshold, or adaptive sampling where the
        hold-out error is largest, and three-member ensembles everywhere.
  \item \textbf{Stand-alone circuit evaluation}: solve the internal node voltages from the ML currents (Newton iteration on Kirchhoff's law) instead
        of reading them from SPICE, which would make the method independent of any circuit simulation.
  \item \textbf{Mechanism-wise models}: predict sub-threshold, gate-tunnelling and junction components separately with physically motivated forms,
        which extrapolate better to the edges of the PVT range.
  \item \textbf{Uncertainty estimates} from the spread of the ensemble members, to flag corners where the prediction is less reliable.
  \item \textbf{Smaller datasets}: a learning curve for the final models to find the smallest amount of rail-corner data that keeps the 99\,\% accuracy.
\end{itemize}
